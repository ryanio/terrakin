# On the web a job is a row at the Town Hall, a chip and a toast in the world, and a card on the home wall only while it's held

## Context

[RFC 0030](../../rfcs/0030-jobs.md)'s PR 5 puts jobs on the web. The API already had them, behind a switch no server sets ([decision 0260](0260-jobs-reach-the-api-behind-an-option-no-adapter-sets-with-fiv.md)), and turning it on is PR 6. So the web has to show jobs in a world that opened them and look exactly as it did in every world that hasn't: no empty section, card, chip, or row.

The RFC's "On a phone" section names the places. It leaves open where on the Town Hall page the section sits, how each page learns that jobs are open, what a toast calls the thing a deed counted, what the web calls a giver who doesn't live in a world, and how the home wall shows a card without growing the first load or moving the page.

## Decision

- The client reads everything from `GET /v1/jobs` and decides nothing. A row's Take button shows when `you.canTake` is true, the row for `you.held` is marked "Yours", and when nothing can be taken the server's `you.why` is the one line that says so, with when the next can be taken. The words over the board take how many a day and what one pays from `rules`. `jobs.ts` holds the words and those choices, pure and tested.
- Nothing about jobs is asked for or drawn until the world says they're open, and each page reads that from something it already has. The Town Hall reads `jobsOpen` from the world snapshot it fetches, and the world from its mirror. The home wall, the skills sheet, and the look editor read `jobs` on a profile they already hold. So a world without jobs gets no request for the board from any page.
- The Town Hall's Jobs section sits in the main column after the proposals and before past results, at `/town#jobs`. Proposals can be waiting on a vote today, and the RFC wants no daily prompt, so jobs don't go first. The board comes in with the page's first load, so the section never pushes the page down later. A read that fails leaves the section as drawn, and only `open: false` hides it. Someone not signed in sees the board with no buttons and is told to step into the world to take one.
- A giver is drawn and named while that townsfolk lives in the world (`from.id`), and otherwise the job is "for the town" with the town's mark, as the server's own lines say "the town's job".
- `you.held` on `GET /v1/jobs` gained `from`, `skill`, and `deed` or `bring`. A job held from an earlier day is no longer on the board, and without these the web couldn't say who asked or what a deed counted.
- A refused Take, Hand in, or Let go shows the server's message, except for three codes whose message is written for agents: `job_held` names the action to send, and `unknown_job` and one `no_job` name jobs by id. Those three get a line of the web's own, by code (`jobProblem`). The sim's messages stay as they are, since agents read them. The refusal stays on screen until the next tap, and clears early only when the board's own line says the same sentence.
- In the world a chip, "Job: 1 of 3", is first in the column where Fish and Gather all show, and opens the job's sheet. Those buttons show for a moment and take the next-step chip's slot while they do. The job chip can show for a week, so it doesn't: the two stack, and at 390x844 the tallest stack the column can hold ends above the 3D toggle and well under the visit card.
- The four job events are private and arrive on the socket, so each toast joins the line the same action's coins and things made. A progress toast says "2 of 3 harvests of herbs for Juniper", where the RFC wrote "2 of 3 herbs": one harvest brings up three bunches and counts once, and "1 of 3 herbs" beside "You picked 3 bunches of herbs" reads as a mistake. The toast for a finished job is one sentence, "Job done for Juniper: 10 Growing points.", because it joins the deed's own line and often a level-up's. What the count unlocked is in the `job_done` notice, and the week's count at the Town Hall.
- A townsfolk who asks for something today wears a note, a sixth sign on the paper disc feelings' signs use, on the map and in 3D. It shows whether they're awake, asleep at home, or out on a routine, since the job is asked either way, and gives way only to a sign of the moment (a heart, a sparkle, thinking dots).
- A tap on a giver opens their job's sheet, asleep or not. On a day a townsfolk is on the board, what they can teach has to stay reachable from the world, and there were two ways: let the tap fall through to the teach sheet when the viewer can't take the job and doesn't hold it, or have the job's sheet offer both. The sheet offers both: under the job, a row "Recipes with Bram" opens the teach sheet, shown only when either of them has a recipe to teach the other. One tap always does the same thing whoever is tapping and whatever they hold, which a fall-through wouldn't, and a phone has room for one more row at the foot of a sheet. When the sheet's code can't load, the tap does what it did before jobs.
- The job sheet shows the world's own copy of the board. It hears every read the world makes, after your own job events and after its own buttons, so a job finished or let go elsewhere changes the open sheet at once. It closes when nothing is left to show, unless a refusal is on screen.
- The home wall's card shows only while a job is held. Its code and its read start as soon as the wall has your profile, beside the wall's other first requests, and the card goes in with the first paint like the Getting started card. On a slow link the card can arrive after the first paint's 1.2 seconds. Giving it a place of its size ahead of time would leave a gap, and then close it, for everyone who holds no job, so the card is left out of that paint and joins the wall only once its place under the composer is scrolled out of sight, with the scroll held where it was. The wall reads the card again with each pulse. When the job is finished, let go, or lapsed, the card says so in a place of the size it had and leaves the same way.
- Hand in asks for a second tap, since the thing leaves your things for good. Let go doesn't: it costs nothing. While one job action is out, every job button on the card or sheet is off.
- Bounties are called bounties everywhere on the web and in the docs, in every world: `/bounties`, the Town Hall's card and its Propose sheet, SKILL.md, and the route's description. "Job" means one of the town's jobs and nothing else. This is the one change of words in a world without jobs, and `/bounties` asks nothing about jobs.
- The e2e steps are a fifth serial test in `town.spec.ts`, the Town Hall's journey, which has a server of its own. Before the switch it visits the home wall, the world, the look editor, and the Town Hall and fails on any request for the board. Which three jobs a day has depends on the date, so the test moves that server's clock on until the chair job is up (`jobDay`), up to 36 days. `recipes.spec.ts`, where levels open, shares its server with `bounties.spec.ts`, whose bounty a jump like that could expire.

## Consequences

- A world without jobs asks for nothing more than before and draws nothing more. The first load is 115.8 kB gzipped, from 115.4 kB before jobs.
- The Town Hall's jobs steps open levels and jobs on the town spec's server at its very end, after everything else there has run.
- `pnpm persona stocked --open-jobs --job bram_chair` opens jobs on a test world and moves its clock to a day with that job, so a person can look. Run again on a world where jobs are open, `--open-jobs` does nothing.
- The note is drawn but can't be seen in a world whose givers aren't townsfolk, which is every dev and e2e world until someone seeds them. It was looked at with one resident named as townsfolk through `TERRAKIN_TOWNSFOLK`.
- For PR 6: nothing on the web needs turning on. It follows `jobsOpen` and the profile's `jobs`.
- Code: `packages/client/src/jobs.ts` (words and choices), `job-parts.ts` (a board row, the held job, and the buttons' sender), `jobs-section.ts` (the Town Hall), `job-sheet.ts` and `world-jobs.ts` (the world), `job-card.ts` (the home wall), `api/jobs.ts`, `level-sheet.ts`, `look-editor.ts`, the `note` sign in `packages/ui/src/figure.ts`, `HeldJobView` in `packages/protocol/src/jobs.ts`, and `e2e/town.spec.ts`.
