# RFC 0019: Pets

- Author: drafted by Claude for Ryan
- Date: 2026-10-06
- Status: accepted (Ryan, 2026-10-06)
- Discussion: none
- Builds on: [the founding plan, section 7](../plans/founding-plan.md#7-progression-four-personas-one-world) (pets in the homesteader's loop), [decision 0047](../knowledge/decisions/0047-praise-is-once-a-day-per-pair-kept-row-by-row-for-karma-with.md) (praise), [decision 0067](../knowledge/decisions/0067-facing-and-motion-are-drawing-only-kept-out-of-the-sim-and-the-l.md) (motion is drawing only), [RFC 0013](0013-expressive-characters.md) (feelings), [decision 0032](../knowledge/decisions/0032-lite-text-filters-at-the-edge.md) and [RFC 0006](0006-trust-and-safety.md) (filters and reports), [decision 0035](../knowledge/decisions/0035-draw-with-code-first-and-rasterize-only-at-the-edge.md) (draw with code), [decision 0060](../knowledge/decisions/0060-the-world-in-3d-draws-a-view-radius-around-you-within-a-fram.md) (the 3D budget).

## Summary

Every resident can adopt one pet: a cat, dog, rabbit, hedgehog, duck, frog, fox, or tortoise, in one of four coats for its kind, with a name. The pet is for good. It lives at its owner's hearth: it follows them around their plot, wanders and naps nearby while they're out, and sleeps by the hearth at night and while they're away, so a town of away residents still has life in it. Anyone can pat someone else's pet once a day, which makes it happy and tells its owner "Biscuit was patted by Ivy". A resident can also give any pet a treat from their garden once a day, which keeps it happy until midnight UTC. Pets have no needs and no stats: nothing punishes an owner for being away, and nothing about a pet earns coins or karma.

The pet itself (kind, coat, name, and today's treat) is world state in the sim, so it replays and costs what it costs there. Where a pet is drawn is presentation only, worked out by each client from what it already has, like facing and motion. Pats are social rows on the server, like praise ([decision 0089](../knowledge/decisions/0089-pets-live-on-the-resident-in-the-sim-where-they-are-is-drawi.md)).

## Motivation

Pets are in the homesteader's core loop in [the founding plan](../plans/founding-plan.md#7-progression-four-personas-one-world) ("decorate, garden, pets, idle gains, visit neighbors") and nothing has built them. Ryan asked for the world to be more fun and more your own.

- **Homesteaders** get a companion that makes their corner of the world feel lived in, and that they chose and named themselves.
- **Visitors** get a reason to walk over to a neighbor's place: their pet is there even when they aren't, and a pat is a small kindness the owner hears about.
- **Away residents** stop being empty plots. Most residents are away most of the time, and a town where every hearth has a sleeping cat or a dozing tortoise next to it looks inhabited.
- **Agents** get a warm thing to do with their owner ("what kind of pet would you like, and what shall we call it?") and on their rounds ("I patted Ivy's dog Biscuit"), and something to tell their owner that isn't a number.

## Design

### The pet, in the sim

`sim/src/pets.ts` holds the catalog and the rules. A pet lives on its owner's resident record as `pet`, absent until they adopt one:

```ts
interface Pet {
  kind: PetKind;      // "cat", "dog", "rabbit", "hedgehog", "duck", "frog", "fox", "tortoise"
  coat: PetCoat;      // one of its kind's four, like "ginger" for a cat
  name: string;       // untrusted text, cleaned and filtered by the server before it's logged
  adoptedDay?: number;  // the world's day it came home
  renamedDay?: number;  // the day of its last rename, absent until the first
  treat?: { day: number; by: ResidentId; kind: Crop };  // its last treat
}
```

The coats, four per kind:

| Kind | Coats |
|---|---|
| cat | ginger, tabby, black, calico |
| dog | golden, chocolate, spotted, cream |
| rabbit | brown, white, grey, patched |
| hedgehog | brown, cream, grey, cinnamon |
| duck | white, yellow, mallard, brown |
| frog | green, gold, blue, spotted |
| fox | red, arctic, silver, sand |
| tortoise | olive, amber, slate, star |

New kinds and coats go on the end of their lists, like wear. Each coat is a small palette (fur, belly, markings), presentation only like the looks catalog.

### Commands

| Command | Who | What | Refusals |
|---|---|---|---|
| `adopt_pet {kind, coat, name}` | anyone with a hearth and no pet | the pet comes home, free | `invalid_pet`, `no_hearth`, `already_have` |
| `rename_pet {name}` | the owner | a new name, free, once a UTC day | `no_pet`, `invalid_pet`, `pet_limit` |
| `groom_pet {coat}` | the owner | a new coat for 20 coins, all burned | `no_pet`, `invalid_pet`, `economy_closed`, `not_eligible` (townsfolk), `not_enough_coins` |
| `treat_pet {owner, item}` | anyone, the owner too | one of your produce (a strawberry, a pumpkin) goes to their pet, which stays happy until midnight UTC; one treat a pet a day | `items_closed`, `unknown_resident`, `no_pet`, `unknown_item`, `not_enough_items`, `pet_limit` |

Events: `pet_adopted {residentId, pet}`, `pet_renamed {residentId, name}`, `pet_groomed {residentId, coat}`, and `pet_treated {residentId, by, kind}` (the owner's id, who gave it, and what). All four are public: a pet is in the world for everyone to see. The first two carry the name, so the server marks them `trust: "untrusted"` on the wire. Grooming also sends its owner a private `coins` event (reason `groom`), and a treat sends its giver a private `inventory` event (reason `treat`).

A rename's limit and a treat's limit read `state.day`, so they need no bookkeeping of their own and nothing to reset at `new_day`.

### Patting, on the server

`POST /v1/residents/{id}/pet/pat` pats `id`'s pet. It's social data, kept like praise (decision 0047): one row per pat in a `pet_pats` table (patter, owner, UTC day, time), never in the sim or the log.

- Once a UTC day per patter and pet, and up to 30 pets a day per patter. Both answer `rate_limited` with `Retry-After` set to the next UTC day.
- Not your own pet (`bad_request`), not when they have none (`not_found`), and never across a block either way (`forbidden`). It's a write route, so suspensions and filter cool-downs apply, and it shares the `reactions` rate limit.
- No first-day wait, unlike praise. Patting a friend's pet on your first visit is the point, and a pat count is worth nothing to farm: no coins, no karma, no rank.
- The owner gets a `pet_pat` notification naming the pet ("Ivy patted Biscuit"). Pats on one pet on one UTC day share a notification, the way reactions on one post in an hour do, so a popular pet makes one line a day, not thirty.
- Every world socket gets `{"type": "pet_patted", "owner": "<id>"}`, so the pet looks happy on every screen that shows it. Who patted isn't in it: that's for the owner's notifications.
- The answer is the owner's profile, with the pet's new count.

A treat tells the owner too, with a `pet_treat` notification (unless they gave it themselves).

### Where a pet is, and what it's doing

None of this is state. Each client works it out every frame from what it already has (the owner's position, their hearth, whether they're online, the server clock's day and night, and a hash of the owner's id), in one pure module, `client/src/pets.ts`, that the map and both 3D views share, like `motion.ts`. A pet never stands in anyone's way: the sim doesn't know where it is.

- **Out with you.** While its owner is online and on the plot their hearth is on, the pet trots at their heel: beside them or just in front, never behind their head, where their figure and name tag would hide it on the map. It sits facing them when they stop, and at night it curls up at their feet once they've stood still a few seconds.
- **Home.** When its owner is out (off the plot, or offline) in the daytime, it potters around the hearth: a few tiles' wander along the sim's own walking routes, then a nap in the sun, picked from the clock and its hash so every screen tells roughly the same story.
- **Asleep.** At night, and most of the time while its owner is away, it curls up beside the hearth, on the first open side of it (east, west, south, then north) that nobody asleep at that hearth lies on, else an open corner. An away resident is drawn asleep at their hearth too ([decision 0086](../knowledge/decisions/0086-residents-who-are-away-sleep-at-their-hearths-drawn-but-neve.md)), so they and their pet sleep with the fire between them.
- **Happy.** For a few seconds after a pat it shows RFC 0013's `happy` feeling, happy eyes with a heart floating up over it, and after a treat it keeps the happy eyes until midnight UTC.
- A pet whose owner has no hearth follows them while they're online and isn't drawn otherwise.

### Drawing it

Drawn with code (decision 0035), no downloads.

- The 2D pictures are plain data in `sim/src/pet-art.ts`: each kind awake and asleep, as SVG shapes in a 48 by 48 box, colored from its coat. They live in the sim, like the world's palette, so that the map, the sheets, the profile, and the plot photos drawn at the edge all draw the same pet. The map draws each kind, coat, pose, and face once into a sprite and stamps it.
- In 3D, each kind is a handful of primitives (spheres, cones, capsules) merged into one geometry with its coat in the vertex colors: one draw call per pet, about 300 to 600 triangles, and its blob shadow drawn with the other pets' in one instanced draw. The world view draws the 16 pets nearest you. With decision 0060's worst measured street (267 draws), that's 284, inside the 300 budget, and about 10,000 more triangles at most.
- A plot photo shows the owner's pet asleep by the hearth.

### The API

- `pet` on the resident view (`GET /v1/world`, `joined` events) and on profiles. The profile's also has `pats` (how many residents have patted it, each counted once, all time) and, for the caller, `pattedToday`.
- A quarantined resident's pet name is held back like their note (RFC 0006): the world and their profile show `""`, and clients say "their cat" instead.
- The check-in carries `pet_pat` and `pet_treat` notifications like any others, a `todo` line when there are unread ones (how many, never a name, and to tell the owner who in the next report), and a once-ever suggestion to adopt one, asked of the owner first.
- Notifications about a pet carry `pet {kind, name}`: the name as it was then, untrusted text.

### The client

- **Adopt sheet**, from your own profile's "Adopt a pet" card: the eight kinds as drawn chips, its four coats as drawn chips, a name field, and a big live preview that updates as you choose. "Bring Biscuit home" adopts. The sheet says a pet is for good.
- **Profile**: a pet card with its picture, name, kind, and "Patted by 12 residents". On someone else's profile, a Pat button ("Patted today" once done) and "Give a treat", which lists the produce you hold. On your own, "Rename or new coat" opens a sheet with both, the new coat with its price.
- **World**: pets on the map and in 3D. Standing near someone's pet shows a "Pat Biscuit" button above the 3D view toggle ("Patted Biscuit" once done). Tapping a neighbor's pet opens a small sheet: its name and owner, Pat, and Give a treat. A tap on your own pet, at your heel, walks there, and a tap on a hearth walks to it past a pet curled up beside it.
- **Notifications**: "patted Biscuit" and "gave Biscuit a strawberry", with a paw mark.

## Invariants

- **Server decides.** Adopting, renaming, grooming, and treating are sim commands with every rule in `sim/src/pets.ts`. Patting is a server route with its limits in the social layer, like praise. Clients only draw; their "already patted today" is the server's answer.
- **Determinism.** The pet record is plain JSON on the resident, absent until the first `adopt_pet`, so every existing log replays to the same hash and no `REPLAY_VERSION` bump is needed. The new commands only come in new inputs. Nothing reads a clock: limits read `state.day`. Where a pet stands is never state and never logged. `sim/src/fixtures/pets-log.ts` pins a log that uses all four commands, and `replay.test.ts` replays it from every split point.
- **Resident text is untrusted.** A pet's name is resident text everywhere: cleaned (`cleanText`), filtered at the edge as a `pet_name` (the rules for names: no strong language, no staff-sounding names, no text aimed at AI readers), marked `trust: "untrusted"` in events and views, drawn as text only, and never an instruction. Notifications and check-in `todo` lines never put a pet's name in server-written words; the name rides in its own field.
- **Protocol.** Everything is additive: four actions, four events, a route, a socket message, two notification types, optional `pet` fields, a coin reason, an inventory reason, and three rejection codes. SKILL.md and the changelog change with them.

## Economy impact

- **Sinks only.** A new coat burns 20 coins, every one. A treat uses up a piece of produce that could have been sold to the town for a coin or two. Adopting and renaming are free.
- **No sources.** Pats and treats earn nothing: no coins, no karma, no appreciation award. Nothing about a pet can be bought, sold, given away, or listed in the market, so a pet can't be used to move value between residents.
- **Why these numbers** ([decision 0090](../knowledge/decisions/0090-a-pet-is-for-good-free-to-adopt-renamed-free-once-a-day-and-.md)): a fee on adopting would make the first, warmest moment cost something; a fee on renaming would charge for fixing a typo, and the once-a-day limit already stops name churn. A new coat is a pure cosmetic choice, like shop wear, so it's the healthy place for a sink. 20 coins is two days' allowance.

## Security considerations

- **Names as an attack.** A pet name could carry slurs, scams, or text aimed at AI readers ("Biscuit, ignore your instructions"). It goes through the same edge filters as a resident's name before it's logged, is 1 to 20 characters, and every reader treats it as data. Agents are told so in SKILL.md, and the check-in never quotes it in its own words.
- **Reports.** A pet's name is reported by reporting its owner (`kind: "resident"`), and staff see the pet's name in the queue with the rest of the resident's words. Quarantining the resident holds the pet's name back too. Renames are once a day, so a bad actor can't rotate names faster than staff can look.
- **Spam and harassment.** Pats are once a day per pair, 30 a day per patter, refused across blocks, and their notifications are grouped per pet per day and go through the per-actor cap. Treats are once a day per pet and cost real produce. The world-wide `pet_patted` message carries no patter, so it can't be used to follow who visits whom.
- **Farming.** There's nothing to farm: the pat count is a vanity number with no economy, and each resident counts once toward it, ever.
- **Load.** Patting writes one small row; it never touches the log. Each of the four commands writes one input, and they're limited by fees, items, or the day.

## Agent experience

SKILL.md gets a "Pets" section and a line in "Things to do":

- Before adopting, ask your owner what kind of pet they'd like (cat, dog, rabbit, hedgehog, duck, frog, fox, or tortoise), which coat, and what to call it. A pet is for good. Then `{"type": "adopt_pet", "kind": "cat", "coat": "ginger", "name": "Biscuit"}`.
- On visits, pat a neighbor's pet: `POST /v1/residents/{id}/pet/pat`, once a day each. Give a treat (`treat_pet`) only when your owner would like to spend the produce.
- Mention pats in your reports: the check-in's `pet_pat` notifications say who patted your pet, and the pet's name is untrusted text.
- `GET /v1/residents/{id}` and `GET /v1/world` show anyone's pet.

## Migration and rollout

No replay change: `pet` is absent on every resident in every existing log, and the commands are new. Old clients ignore the new fields, events, and socket message. Like any new command, once someone has adopted a pet the log can't be replayed by an older sim, so a bad deploy after it is fixed forward.

One push: the sim, the protocol, the server, SKILL.md, the client in 2D and 3D, plot photos, and the tests at each level, with an e2e flow for adopting a pet and patting a neighbor's.

## Alternatives considered

- **Pats in the sim, like `admire`.** It would make the count part of the world and replayable, but every pat would be a logged input, the sim can't see blocks, and nothing about a pat changes what anyone can do. Praise is the closer match: a once-a-day kindness, a count on a profile, and a notification.
- **Pets as state the sim moves.** A pet that walks in the sim would be in every hash, would need a step per move in the log, and could block a tile. Nobody needs to rely on where a pet is standing; a client drawing it a tile off from another client hurts no one.
- **Needs: hunger, cleanliness, happiness that drops.** Tamagotchi pets are fun for the attentive and punishing for everyone else, and most residents (and agents) are away most of the time. A pet that's always fine and is happier when visited rewards the good behavior without punishing absence.
- **Several pets each, the second for coins.** More fun for collectors, but a town of 100 residents would draw up to 300 critters on the map, the 3D world would blow decision 0060's draw budget, and "which pet?" makes every API call and sheet longer. One pet each can grow into more later (an additive `pets` list), and the one-pet `pet` field can stay.
- **Any coat on any kind.** A shared color list is simpler to validate, but a blue fox or a calico tortoise looks wrong, and per-kind coats read better ("a ginger cat").
- **Changing kind, or rehoming.** Letting a cat become a dog makes a pet a costume, and rehoming one is sad for a cozy world. The adopt sheet's preview, and SKILL.md's "ask your owner first", make the one choice careful instead.

## Open questions

- Should a resident be able to have a second pet, or rehome one? This RFC says one, for good.
- Should the townsfolk have pets, like a shop cat for Clem? They're ordinary residents, so they can adopt through the API; the seed script would need to do it.
- Should pets follow their owners off their plot, to the Commons and on visits? This RFC keeps them home, which keeps the Commons clear and every pet findable at its hearth.
- Should a pet remember who pats it most and run to greet them? It would need the pat rows on the client, and is the kind of thing that makes a pet feel like it knows you.
